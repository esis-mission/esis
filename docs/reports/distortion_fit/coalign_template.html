ESIS-I channels on the sky, through the fit

Each channel's Level-1 frame read onto one sky grid through its fitted distortion at the chosen line, with the frame's pointing and window drift applied, block-averaged to a few arcseconds. Every image is scaled to a mean of one over the pixels all four channels cover, so intensities and differences are in units of the mean. The high-pass version subtracts a copy blurred with a 21″ Gaussian, removing the vignetting and effective-area gradients while keeping every structure smaller than a supergranule; it is zero-mean by construction. Left: blink between channels A and B. Right: A − B, blue where A is darker and red where brighter, saturating at the chosen fraction of the mean. The arrow in each image points along that channel's dispersion on the sky: a redshifted feature is displaced along it, so in A − B a Doppler shift appears as a paired deficit and excess along the two arrows while stationary structure cancels.
